About

About Me

I work at the intersection of political science, public policy, and computational social science. I hold a PhD (2025) from KU Leuven, where I studied the career backgrounds of ministerial advisers through a text-as-data lens — research that took me to the MIT Gov Lab and the University of Nottingham. I also hold a Master's degree in Artificial Intelligence from KU Leuven, specialising in speech and language technology.

I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Antwerp, working on DEMO-LIES — a large-scale project mapping accusations of lying in parliamentary debates across more than 20 countries using NLP and corpus methods.

This site documents these projects and my broader work as a research associate at the Oxford Computational Political Science Group and as a contributor to the COST Action CoREx WG2, which is building the first comparative dataset and handbook on political and administrative elites.

Projects

A showcase of research and tools built at the intersection of Political Science and AI.

DEMO-LIES

As a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Antwerp, I contribute to this ERC-funded project that maps accusations of lying in parliamentary debates across more than 20 countries — combining large-scale text corpora with NLP classification to study democratic discourse at scale.

NLP Parliamentary Debates Comparative Politics

PhD Research: In and Out the Centre of Executive Power

My dissertation maps the career trajectories of Belgian ministerial advisers using NLP and text-as-data methods. By tracing the paths of hundreds of political elites, it sheds light on who gains access to executive power — and what their backgrounds reveal about the nature of ministerial cabinets in Belgium.

Political Science NLP Career Analysis
View Project

US Political Appointees (2004–2020)

Developed during a research stay at the MIT Gov Lab, this project extends the software built during my PhD to construct a background dataset of US presidential appointees between 2004 and 2020. Using LLM-powered tools, we automatically collected and structured data on their education and career trajectories from online sources.

US Politics LLMs Automated Pipeline
View Project

LLMs for Career Data

Can LLMs reliably help us with collecting, extracting and annotating elite data? This project develops and evaluates LLM-based pipelines for automating career data collection and annotation — from web-scraping to structured classification — drastically reducing the cost of large-N elite studies.

LLMs Career Analysis Automation Elite Studies
View Project

Oxford Computational Political Science Group

As a Research Associate at the Oxford Computational Political Science Group, I contribute to a project benchmarking LLMs for the classification of parliamentary speech. We systematically test a range of models across multiple corpora to understand how well LLMs perform on annotation tasks in parliamentary data.

LLMs Benchmarking Parliamentary Data
